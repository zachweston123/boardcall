New Smyrna Beach

A jetty break in Florida. Here is when it tends to be good, and what to know before you go.

When it works

New Smyrna Beach picks up swell coming from the north round to the south. It's cleanest when the wind blows from the west-southwest, which is offshore here and smooths the waves.

The wave

A jetty break. Waves break off a jetty or pier, which shapes the sandbars around it.

Getting there and crowds

New Smyrna has more recorded shark bites than anywhere else in the world — almost all are minor hit-and-run nips rather than serious attacks, but it's genuinely worth knowing, especially surfing near the inlet. You can still drive and park right on the hard-packed sand at several public approaches, though visitors pay for a ParkVolusia beach-driving pass and the off-beach lots are charged separately on top of it. The break gets crowded fast, particularly around the jetty on weekends.
