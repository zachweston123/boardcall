Break guide · Santa Cruz, CA

Steamer Lane

A point break in Santa Cruz, CA. Here is when it tends to be good, and what to know before you go.

When it works

Steamer Lane picks up swell coming from the south round to the northwest. It's cleanest when the wind blows from the northeast, which is offshore here and smooths the waves.

The wave

A point break. Waves wrap around a headland and peel along it.

Getting there and crowds

Park along West Cliff Drive near the lighthouse — the lots fill fast on weekends and after school lets out. Entry is down a stairway to the rocks. The cliff above the lineup has shed repeated rock falls in recent years, including one that dropped rubble into the takeoff zone at the Slot, so stay back from the taped-off edge up top and give the inside room at lower tides. This is an experienced lineup with a real pecking order, not a place to learn. Water stays cold year-round and Santa Cruz sits inside the "Red Triangle," so a full wetsuit is standard and shark sightings aren't unheard of.

Etiquette

Steamer Lane has a long, well-documented local surf culture, and lineup conflicts do happen. Watch the lineup before paddling out, respect wave priority, ease into the crowd rather than beelining for the main peak, and if you can, go with someone who knows the break your first time.
