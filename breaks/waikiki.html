Waikiki

A reef break in Honolulu, Oahu, HI. Here is when it tends to be good, and what to know before you go.

When it works

Waikiki picks up swell coming from the south-southeast round to the southwest. It's cleanest when the wind blows from the north, which is offshore here and smooths the waves.

The wave

A reef break. Waves break over reef or rock, so the bottom is shallow and hard.

Getting there and crowds

There's no big surf lot — you're parking in a hotel garage or feeding a street meter, then walking out through the beach in front of the hotels. Canoes (the main beginner wave) has a sandy bottom and is forgiving, but it's also the most congested lineup in Hawaii, packed with longboard rentals, outrigger canoes, and catamarans all day, every day — Publics or Pops nearby offer a bit more room if you want to dodge the traffic. Water's warm year-round so no wetsuit needed, but the other Waikiki breaks (Publics, Populars, Queens) sit over reef rather than sand, and Publics in particular is shallow, sharp and not a beginner wave.
