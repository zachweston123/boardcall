Malibu (Surfrider)

A point break in Los Angeles, CA. Here is when it tends to be good, and what to know before you go.

When it works

Malibu (Surfrider) picks up swell coming from the south round to the west. It's cleanest when the wind blows from the north, which is offshore here and smooths the waves.

The wave

A point break. Waves wrap around a headland and peel along it.

Getting there and crowds

Free parking along PCH is limited (no parking 12am–5am) or pay roughly $10/day in the lot by the pier. It's known for one of SoCal's more territorial lineups, especially at First Point, so newcomers should sit wide and watch the pecking order before paddling for waves; it's busiest 9am–1pm with locals and surf schools, quieter around sunset.

Etiquette

Surfrider is one of the most iconic breaks in California and also one of the most crowded. Locals have a well-documented reputation for territorial behavior here — including a mainstream travel-press warning as recently as early 2026 — though First Point is simultaneously one of the friendliest, most surf-school-heavy spots in SoCal for beginners. Watch the lineup from the beach for a few minutes before paddling out, ease into the pack gradually rather than heading straight for the main peak, respect wave priority, and communicate clearly — most friction here comes from crowding and etiquette breakdowns rather than any single person's attitude.
