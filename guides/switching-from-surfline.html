Switching From Surfline? Here's What Changes, and What You Gain

Surfline is a great way to look at the ocean. It is a worse way to decide. You open it, scan the rating, read the height, check the wind, glance at the swell rose and the tide curve, and then you do the actual work yourself: is this worth it for me, today, at my level, on the boards I own? Boardcall starts where Surfline stops. It reads the same swell, wind and tide, then gives you the answer in one line and names the board to grab. Everything you are used to checking is still there, one scroll down, in the same order. This page shows you where it went and what you get on top.

What Boardcall does that Surfline doesn't

  • It picks the board. Surfline's best effort is a line like “Ride an all-rounder in 2-3ft waves.” Boardcall knows the boards you actually own — length, width, thickness, volume — and names the one to bring today, with the reason. That is the whole product, and no rating scale can give it to you.
  • The verdict is about you. Surfline's rating is a spot's potential for a generic surfer, and their own support pages say so. Boardcall's verdict is for you: it is capped by a safety gate at your skill level and by whether your best board floats you in today's surf. When it holds a day back, it tells you why, in a line under the badge.
  • It writes the report instead of making you read one. Surfline's forecaster write-ups are regional, Premium, and updated a few times a day. Boardcall writes a two-sentence briefing for your break every morning, free, in plain English, sizes in body parts.
  • It tells you when. Best time and Also good are computed for your break, bounded to daylight, every day, not just when a forecaster gets to your region.
  • No account, no location, no ads. Boardcall never asks who you are or where you are. Surfline's free tier opens on an ad; Boardcall opens on the call.

The rating: Poor to Epic becomes Skip to Fire

Surfline uses seven words drawn as five bars. Boardcall uses four, drawn as a coloured badge with a small colour scale under it so you can see where today sits at a glance. The mapping is roughly:

  • Very Poor / Poor → Skip (not today)
  • Poor to Fair → Marginal (only if you're keen)
  • Fair → Fun (worth going)
  • Fair to Good → Fun or Fire
  • Good / Very Good / Epic → Fire (drop everything)

They will disagree sometimes, and when they do Boardcall is usually telling you something Surfline can't. A Fair day at a heavy reef is a Skip for a beginner. A small clean day is a Fun on a longboard and a Marginal on a 5'10". Surfline rates the wave; Boardcall rates your session.

Surf height: one honest number instead of a range

Surfline shows a range (“2-3 ft”) with a body-part label. Boardcall shows one face-height number (“2.6 ft”) with the same kind of label (“knee to waist high”). Boardcall's number is calibrated against real reports to land just under the top of Surfline's range for the same hour, so you can read it as roughly Surfline's bigger number, without the hedge. Where a NOAA buoy sits offshore of your break, Boardcall also shows its live measured reading on a separate Buoy row, so you can see the model and the measurement side by side instead of taking either on faith.

Wind, swell, tide, water: same rows, same order, fewer decoder rings

Scroll past the briefing and the board card and you are in a conditions panel that runs in Surfline's own order: Size, Swell, Wind swell, Energy, Wind, Tide, Water, Daylight. The differences are deliberate:

  • Wind reads “13 kt NNW · cross-shore · textured” — the direction word you scan for, plus what it is doing to the face. Surfline gives you the first and leaves the second to you.
  • Swell shows the primary swell and the wind swell as two rows rather than three components and a compass rose. The Energy figure is total offshore energy with a plain-words band next to it (“punchy”), because a kJ number on its own is one more thing to decode.
  • Tide shows the current height, direction, and the next high and low; tap it for the 24-hour curve. Size, Energy and Wind open charts the same way.
  • Daylight lists first light and last light, not just sunrise and sunset, and the best-time windows respect them.

Hourly ratings and the day's window

Surfline's hourly bar is a row of coloured ratings. Boardcall's hour strip does the same job and more: each hour is tinted with your verdict for that hour and shows the wind, the face height and the size words. Under it, Best time and Also good name the windows, the same “dawn to 10am” call a forecaster write-up gives, computed for your break every day.

Favorites and the map

Your saved breaks sit as a chip row at the top of the Map tab, each with its verdict and size, the favourites list you are used to opening on. Then the map does something Surfline's doesn't: it rates every one of 336 built-in breaks for your quiver and calls out the best one near you right now. Tap any pin for that break's full conditions and its local knowledge notes.

What you're trading, honestly

Three things Surfline has that Boardcall doesn't, so you can decide with your eyes open.

  • Cams. Boardcall has no camera network. Plenty of surfers keep Surfline for the cam and open Boardcall for the call. If the cam is the thing you pay Surfline for, that is a fair reason to keep paying.
  • Observed heights. Surfline stamps a height “Observed” when a forecaster or a Smart Cam has checked it. Boardcall's height is modelled, calibrated, and shown next to a live buoy reading where one exists. We would rather show you the measurement than a tag.
  • A longer outlook. Boardcall's Forecast tab runs seven days, with the board for each. Surfline shows ten free and sixteen on Premium. If you plan trips two weeks out, that is theirs.

Everything else on this page is in Boardcall's free tier. Boardcall Pro only lifts the weekly cap on AI-written briefings; the verdict, the board pick, the map and the forecast never cost anything.

Quick answers

Is Boardcall better than Surfline?

For deciding whether to go and what to ride, yes: that is the only thing Boardcall is built to do, and Surfline does not do it at all. For watching a live cam or planning a trip two weeks out, Surfline is the better tool. Many surfers use both.

Why does Boardcall say Fun when Surfline says Poor to Fair?

Because they answer different questions. Surfline rates the spot's potential for a generic surfer; Boardcall rates whether today is worth it for you, on your boards, at your level. A small, clean day on a longboard is a genuinely fun session even when the spot is not living up to its reputation, and Boardcall will say so.

Is Boardcall's wave height the same as Surfline's?

Close. Boardcall shows a single face-height number calibrated against real reports to land just under the top of Surfline's range for the same hour. Where a NOAA buoy is nearby, the Buoy row shows the measured offshore height beside it, which Surfline's free tier does not put on the spot page.
