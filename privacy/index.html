Boardcall

Privacy Policy

Effective September 14, 2026

Boardcall ("the app," "we," "us") is a surf-briefing app for iPhone and iPad, built and maintained by an independent developer, Zach Weston. This policy describes, plainly, what the app does with data — what stays on your phone, what leaves it, who it goes to, and why. There is no legal team behind this app and no reason to hide anything, so this is meant to be read, not skimmed past.

The short version

  • Boardcall has no accounts at all. There is no sign-up, no sign-in, and no password anywhere in the app.
  • Your profile, your boards, your chosen surf spots and any sessions you log are stored only on your device.
  • Boardcall never uses your location. You choose spots from a built-in list — there is no GPS access anywhere in the app.
  • Checking the forecast sends the coordinates of a spot from that built-in list to our own small server, which fetches and caches the wave and wind data. Nothing about you goes with it.
  • When you tap for an AI-written briefing, the text is written by a third-party AI service (Anthropic's Claude), reached through our server. That request includes the day's conditions, your skill level and weight, and your boards. Our server does not log or store it. Anthropic holds it for up to 30 days on their own systems as an anti-abuse measure.
  • If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro and log sessions, a weekly coaching note is written the same way, from a summary of those sessions and your three most recent session notes.
  • Our server does keep one small, anonymous kind of record: a randomly generated ID plus a daily count, used only to stop abuse of the AI features. It cannot be tied back to you as a person.
  • If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro, Apple handles the entire purchase and payment. We never see your card details or billing information.
  • We do not use analytics, advertising trackers, or crash-reporting tools of any kind.

What data Boardcall collects, and by whom

Different parts of the app handle data differently. Rather than a single blanket statement, here is exactly what happens in each case:

Stored only on your device

Your profile, boards, spots, and app settings

Boardcall keeps private files on your device — ones only Boardcall can read — containing: your surfer profile (skill level, weight, height, and your reminder times), the boards in your quiver (nickname, brand/model, dimensions, volume, type, and fin setup), up to three saved surf spots (chosen from our built-in catalog — never your GPS coordinates), any sessions you log (the date, spot, board, the conditions at the time, your rating, and any notes you type), any coaching notes Boardcall Pro has written for you, whether you've completed onboarding and accepted the Terms, a randomly generated device identifier (explained below), and counts of the AI-written briefings you've used. Boardcall also caches recent wave, wind, tide and buoy data on your device, along with the most recent briefing text for each spot, purely to avoid re-fetching and re-generating it needlessly. It also keeps a small record comparing forecast wave heights with nearby buoy readings, which never leaves your device.

If you add the Boardcall widget, the app keeps a copy of your profile, boards, sessions and home spot in a storage area on your device shared only between the app and its widget, so the widget can show today's call without opening the app. Share cards (a Boardcall Pro feature) are drawn on your device and go only where you choose to send them.

None of this is uploaded to iCloud or synced anywhere by us. (If you use Apple's own device backup features, that backup is between you and Apple, not something Boardcall controls.) Deleting the app deletes all of it, permanently, from your device.

Sent to our server, nothing about you kept

The forecast request

When you check the forecast — on Today, Forecast, the Map, or the widget — the app sends our own small server (built on Supabase, a cloud-hosting platform) the coordinates of a spot from the built-in catalog and which kind of data it needs (waves or wind). Our server fetches that from Open-Meteo using our own paid access, and keeps a copy of the result for up to a day so that everyone checking the same spot shares one request. That copy is forecast data for a map coordinate. It contains nothing about you, no device ID, and no record of who asked.

Sent to our server, not stored

The AI briefing request

Nothing is sent until you tap Get AI briefing on Today. When you do, the app sends a request to our server containing the day's surf conditions at your chosen spot (wave size, swell period, wind, tide, temperature), your skill level and weight, and your boards as ranked by the app's own on-device physics engine. Our server forwards this to Anthropic's Claude — a third-party AI service — to write the plain-English briefing, and returns the result to the app. That content is not written to any database by our server, and on failure our server only logs a generic error code, never the data itself.

Per Anthropic's own API terms, this data is not used to train their models. Anthropic does hold the request and the briefing on their own systems for up to 30 days as an anti-abuse measure, and longer if a request is flagged for review under their usage policies. That retention is theirs, not ours — we never write it to a database and we have no access to what they hold.

Worth knowing: if you give a board a nickname, that nickname is part of what gets sent, because it's how the briefing refers to the board. It's free text you typed, so it's the one field in the request that could contain anything you put there — a plain description like "the yellow one" is all it's designed for.

Sent to our server, not stored

The Boardcall Pro coaching note

If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro and have logged at least three sessions, the app asks our server for a coaching note when you open Today — at most one note a week, and if a request fails, the app tries again no sooner than a day later. The request contains your skill level, how many sessions you've logged, a short summary of the patterns that repeat in them, and your three most recent session notes, word for word. For each board and kind of conditions you've logged at least three times, the summary gives the board's name (including any nickname you gave it), a plain description such as "small, clean days," how many sessions that covers, and your median rating. It does not include the dates or which spots you surfed. If nothing repeats often enough yet, nothing is sent.

Our server forwards the request to Anthropic's Claude to write the note and returns the result. As with the briefing request, it is not written to any database by our server, only generic error codes are logged, and Anthropic's handling is the same as described above.

Session notes are free text you typed, so anything you write in them can end up in this request — if there's something you'd rather not send, leave it out of your notes.

Sent to our server AND stored

The anonymous rate-limit counters

Alongside an AI briefing or coaching-note request, the app sends a random identifier that was generated on your device the first time you opened Boardcall — not your Apple ID, name, email address, advertising ID, or any other identifier Apple or anyone else assigns you. Our server stores that random ID together with the date and a count — one record for briefings and a separate one for coaching notes — and nothing else at the application level, purely to enforce daily limits and prevent abuse of the AI features. These counters are the only application-level records about app users that our infrastructure retains. We delete them once they are more than 30 days old. They cannot be linked back to you as a person — they identify a device-generated random number, not an individual.

Separately, our hosting provider may retain standard technical request logs (such as IP address) for a limited time as part of normal server operation, the way any web server does. We don't access or use those logs for anything beyond troubleshooting an outage.

Handled entirely by Apple

Boardcall Pro subscription payments

If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro, the purchase, payment, and ongoing subscription management happen entirely through Apple's StoreKit system. We never see your card number, billing address, or Apple ID. To determine whether Pro features should be unlocked, the app asks Apple's own StoreKit APIs, live, whether you currently hold an active entitlement — we do not keep our own record of your purchase or subscription status on any server we control.

Location

Boardcall does not use Core Location, does not request location permission, and has no way to know where your device physically is. Every surf spot in the app — on Today, Forecast, or the Map tab — is a fixed point chosen from a built-in catalog, never a GPS reading.

Children's privacy

Boardcall is a surf-forecasting tool for surfers and is not designed for or directed to children. Our Terms of Use ask that you be at least 13 to use it. The app contains no objectionable content, and no account, sign-up, or personal information is required to use any feature — the app has no accounts and no sign-in of any kind. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided us information beyond what's described in this policy, contact us at the email below and we'll delete it.

Your rights and how to delete your data

Because almost everything Boardcall touches lives only on your device, deleting the app deletes your profile, your boards, your saved spots, your logged sessions and coaching notes, and all cached data completely — there is no separate server-side copy of any of that to request or delete.

The only records about you on our server are the anonymous rate-limit counters described above: a random device ID, a date, and a count. We have no way to trace those records back to you as a person, and we delete them once they are more than 30 days old, whether or not you ask.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. There are no advertisers, data brokers, or analytics companies involved in this app at all.

If you still have questions or concerns about your data, email us — see Contact below.

Security

All network requests the app makes, to every service listed above, use HTTPS encryption in transit. The rate-limit and forecast-cache tables described above are not readable by the public: they are reachable only by our own server functions, using credentials that never leave them.

Where this data is handled

Boardcall is run from the United States. Our server runs in a US data center, and the AI provider is a US company. If you use the app from outside the US, the forecast, briefing and coaching-note requests described above go to our server there. The forecast, tide and buoy sources receive only a fixed spot's coordinates, a tide-station ID, or nothing at all — never your profile, boards or sessions. Given how little is involved — no account, no name, no email, no location, and a random ID rather than an identifier tied to you — this is the whole of the international picture.

Changes to this policy

If Boardcall's data practices change, we'll update this page and change the effective date at the top to match. Because the app has no accounts, email addresses, or push notifications tied to a server, there is no separate mechanism for notifying you of changes beyond this page itself — material changes will be described here directly.
