Boardcall

Privacy Policy

Effective November 27, 2026

Boardcall ("the app," "we," "us") is a surf-briefing app for iPhone and iPad, built and maintained by an independent developer, Zach Weston. This policy describes, plainly, what the app does with data — what stays on your phone, what leaves it, who it goes to, and why. There is no legal team behind this app and no reason to hide anything, so this is meant to be read, not skimmed past.

The short version

  • Boardcall has no accounts at all. There is no sign-up, no sign-in, and no password anywhere in the app.
  • Your profile, your boards, your chosen surf spots and any sessions you log are stored only on your device.
  • Boardcall never asks for your location. You choose spots from a built-in list.
  • If you turn on Import surf sessions from Apple Health, Boardcall reads your surf workouts from Apple Health (time, heart rate, calories, water temperature, and, only if you also turn on Include GPS, the workout's route). That data is used on your phone to fill in and score your sessions. It is never sent to our server, to Anthropic, or anywhere else, and it is never used for advertising.
  • Checking the forecast sends the coordinates of a spot from that built-in list to our own small server, which fetches and caches the wave and wind data. Nothing about you goes with it.
  • When you tap for an AI-written briefing, the text is written by a third-party AI service (Anthropic's Claude), reached through our server. That request includes the day's conditions, your skill level and weight, your boards, and your phone's language setting and chosen units (so the briefing comes back in your language and measurements). Our server does not log or store it. Anthropic holds it for up to 30 days on their own systems as an anti-abuse measure.
  • If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro and log sessions, a weekly coaching note is written the same way, from your five most recent sessions (how long ago, the kind of surf, the board, your rating and the session score), patterns across your log, and your three most recent session notes. No Apple Health data is included. The app asks your permission before either kind of AI request is sent.
  • Our server does keep one small, anonymous kind of record: a randomly generated ID plus a daily count, used only to stop abuse of the AI features. It cannot be tied back to you as a person.
  • If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro, Apple handles the entire purchase and payment. We never see your card details or billing information.
  • From version 1.0.15 the app counts which of its features get used (for example, how many times the Week tab was opened) and sends those counts to our server with no identifier of any kind. You can turn this off in the Me tab. We use no third-party analytics, advertising trackers, or crash-reporting tools.

What data Boardcall collects, and by whom

Different parts of the app handle data differently. Rather than a single blanket statement, here is exactly what happens in each case:

Stored only on your device

Your profile, boards, spots, and app settings

Boardcall keeps private files on your device — ones only Boardcall can read — containing: your surfer profile (skill level, weight, height, and your reminder times), the boards in your quiver (nickname, brand/model, dimensions, volume, type, and fin setup), up to three saved surf spots (chosen from our built-in catalog — never your GPS coordinates), any sessions you log (the date, spot, board, the conditions at the time, your rating, any notes you type, and, for a session imported from Apple Health, a summary described in the next section), any coaching notes Boardcall Pro has written for you, whether you've completed onboarding and accepted the Terms, a randomly generated device identifier (explained below), and counts of the AI-written briefings you've used. Boardcall also caches recent wave, wind, tide and buoy data on your device, along with the most recent briefing text for each spot, purely to avoid re-fetching and re-generating it needlessly. It also keeps a small record comparing forecast wave heights with nearby buoy readings, which never leaves your device.

If you add the Boardcall widget, the app keeps a copy of your profile, boards, sessions and home spot in a storage area on your device shared only between the app and its widget, so the widget can show today's call without opening the app. Share cards are drawn on your device and go only where you choose to send them.

None of this is uploaded to iCloud or synced anywhere by us. (If you use Apple's own device backup features, that backup is between you and Apple, not something Boardcall controls.) Deleting the app deletes all of it, permanently, from your device.

Read from Apple Health, kept on your device

Surf sessions from Apple Health

This is off until you turn on Import surf sessions from Apple Health in the Me tab, and iOS then asks you which kinds of Health data Boardcall may read. Boardcall only reads; it never writes anything to Apple Health. It reads surf workouts (such as one recorded by an Apple Watch, or copied into Apple Health by another watch's app) from about the last day and a half, and for each one: when it started and ended, your heart rate during it, active calories, and water temperature. If you also turn on Include GPS, it reads the workout's route, which it uses to estimate how many waves you caught, your longest ride, top speed and distance, and to draw the session on a map.

Nothing is added to your log until you review a session and tap Save. What Boardcall then keeps, in its own files on your device, is the workout's start time and length, your average and highest heart rate, and a short summary (calories, water temperature, distance, top speed, wave count and longest ride). Heart-rate samples and GPS points are read from Apple Health each time you open a session and are never saved by Boardcall.

Health data never leaves your device. It is not sent to our server, it is not part of any AI briefing or coaching-note request, it is not shared with the widget's copy of your data, and it is not included in the anonymous feature counts. We never use it for advertising or marketing, and we never sell it or give it to anyone. You can withdraw Boardcall's access at any time in the iPhone's Settings app, under Health, Data Access & Devices, Boardcall; turning the import off in the Me tab stops Boardcall reading it.

Sent to our server, nothing about you kept

The forecast request

When you check the forecast — on Today, Forecast, the Map, or the widget — the app sends our own small server (built on Supabase, a cloud-hosting platform) the coordinates of a spot from the built-in catalog and which kind of data it needs (waves or wind). Our server fetches that from Open-Meteo using our own paid access, and keeps a copy of the result for up to a day so that everyone checking the same spot shares one request. That copy is forecast data for a map coordinate. It contains nothing about you, no device ID, and no record of who asked.

Sent to our server, not stored

The AI briefing request

Nothing is sent until you tap Get AI briefing on Today. When you do, the app sends a request to our server containing the day's surf conditions at your chosen spot (wave size, swell period, wind, tide, temperature), your skill level and weight, your boards as ranked by the app's own on-device physics engine, and your phone's preferred language and the units you've chosen (feet or metres, °F or °C), so the briefing can be written in that language with those measurements. Our server forwards this to Anthropic's Claude — a third-party AI service — to write the plain-English briefing, and returns the result to the app. That content is not written to any database by our server, and on failure our server only logs a generic error code, never the data itself.

Per Anthropic's own API terms, this data is not used to train their models. Anthropic does hold the request and the briefing on their own systems for up to 30 days as an anti-abuse measure, and longer if a request is flagged for review under their usage policies. That retention is theirs, not ours — we never write it to a database and we have no access to what they hold.

Worth knowing: if you give a board a nickname, that nickname is part of what gets sent, because it's how the briefing refers to the board. It's free text you typed, so it's the one field in the request that could contain anything you put there — a plain description like "the yellow one" is all it's designed for.

Sent to our server, not stored

The Boardcall Pro coaching note

If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro, have logged at least two sessions, and have allowed AI writing, the app asks our server for a coaching note — at most one note a week, and if a request fails, the app tries again no sooner than a day later. The request contains your skill level, how many sessions you've logged, your five most recent sessions, a short summary of the patterns that repeat in your log, and your three most recent session notes, word for word. Each of the five sessions is described by how many days ago it was, a plain description of the surf such as "small, clean days," the day's call, the board you rode (including any nickname you gave it), the board Boardcall would have picked if different, your rating, and the session score; the request also carries your 30-day average score and your best-scoring board. For each board and kind of conditions you've logged at least three times, the summary gives the board's name, the kind of conditions, how many sessions that covers, and your median rating. It does not include calendar dates, which spots you surfed, or anything read from Apple Health, and the session scores it carries are worked out without the time-in-the-water figure that comes from Apple Health.

Our server forwards the request to Anthropic's Claude to write the note and returns the result. As with the briefing request, it is not written to any database by our server, only generic error codes are logged, and Anthropic's handling is the same as described above.

Session notes are free text you typed, so anything you write in them can end up in this request — if there's something you'd rather not send, leave it out of your notes.

Sent to our server AND stored

The anonymous rate-limit counters

Alongside an AI briefing or coaching-note request, the app sends a random identifier that was generated on your device the first time you opened Boardcall — not your Apple ID, name, email address, advertising ID, or any other identifier Apple or anyone else assigns you. Our server stores that random ID together with the date and a count — one record for briefings and a separate one for coaching notes — and nothing else at the application level, purely to enforce daily limits and prevent abuse of the AI features. Apart from the anonymous feature counts described next, these counters are the only application-level records our infrastructure retains. We delete them once they are more than 30 days old. They cannot be linked back to you as a person — they identify a device-generated random number, not an individual.

Separately, our hosting provider may retain standard technical request logs (such as IP address) for a limited time as part of normal server operation, the way any web server does. We don't access or use those logs for anything beyond troubleshooting an outage.

Sent to our server AND stored, with no identifier

Anonymous feature counts

From version 1.0.15, the app keeps a tally on your phone of how often you use each of its features (opening the Week tab, tapping a chart, opening the paywall, finishing a setup page, and so on) and sends those tallies to our own server when you leave the app. Each report carries only the day, the counts, the app version, whether you have Boardcall Pro, the skill level you chose, and roughly how long ago you installed the app (a band such as "2 to 6 days"). It carries no device ID, no account, nothing about which break, board or conditions were involved, and nothing that links one report to another. Our server adds the numbers into daily totals, so what we keep reads like "on November 27, 3 phones opened the tide chart, 5 times in all", and we never see what any one person did. We keep these totals with no set deletion date, because comparing one month with another is what they are for. They are collected by us alone: no third-party analytics company or SDK is involved.

Nothing is sent before you accept the terms in setup. To turn the counts off, switch off Share anonymous usage counts in the Me tab; anything not yet sent is deleted from your phone at that moment. The App Clip counts nothing.

Handled entirely by Apple

Boardcall Pro subscription payments

If you subscribe to Boardcall Pro, the purchase, payment, and ongoing subscription management happen entirely through Apple's StoreKit system. We never see your card number, billing address, or Apple ID. To determine whether Pro features should be unlocked, the app asks Apple's own StoreKit APIs, live, whether you currently hold an active entitlement — we do not keep our own record of your purchase or subscription status on any server we control.

Location

Boardcall does not use Core Location and never asks iOS for location permission. Every surf spot in the app — on Today, Week, or the map — is a fixed point chosen from a built-in catalog, never a GPS reading. The one exception is optional and comes from Apple Health, not from the app: if you turn on Include GPS for imported sessions and allow it in the Health permission sheet, Boardcall reads the GPS route of a surf workout your watch recorded, to draw that session and count its waves. That route is read on your phone, is never saved by Boardcall, and never leaves your device.

Children's privacy

Boardcall is a surf-forecasting tool for surfers and is not designed for or directed to children. Our Terms of Use ask that you be at least 13 to use it. The app contains no objectionable content, and no account, sign-up, or personal information is required to use any feature — the app has no accounts and no sign-in of any kind. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided us information beyond what's described in this policy, contact us at the email below and we'll delete it.

Your rights and how to delete your data

Because almost everything Boardcall touches lives only on your device, deleting the app deletes your profile, your boards, your saved spots, your logged sessions and coaching notes, and all cached data completely — there is no separate server-side copy of any of that to request or delete. Data in Apple Health itself belongs to Apple Health, not Boardcall: deleting Boardcall doesn't remove your workouts from it, and you manage them in the Health app.

The only records about you on our server are the anonymous rate-limit counters described above: a random device ID, a date, and a count. We have no way to trace those records back to you as a person, and we delete them once they are more than 30 days old, whether or not you ask. The anonymous feature counts are not records about you: they carry no identifier, so there is nothing in them we could find or delete for one person. You can stop sending them at any time in the Me tab.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. There are no advertisers, data brokers, or analytics companies involved in this app at all.

If you still have questions or concerns about your data, email us — see Contact below.

Security

All network requests the app makes, to every service listed above, use HTTPS encryption in transit. The rate-limit, feature-count and forecast-cache tables described above are not readable by the public: they are reachable only by our own server functions, using credentials that never leave them.

Where this data is handled

Boardcall is run from the United States. Our server runs in a US data center, and the AI provider is a US company. If you use the app from outside the US, the forecast, briefing and coaching-note requests described above go to our server there. The forecast, tide and buoy sources receive only a fixed spot's coordinates, a tide-station ID, or nothing at all — never your profile, boards or sessions. Given how little is involved — no account, no name, no email, no location, and a random ID rather than an identifier tied to you — this is the whole of the international picture.

Changes to this policy

If Boardcall's data practices change, we'll update this page and change the effective date at the top to match. Because the app has no accounts, email addresses, or push notifications tied to a server, there is no separate mechanism for notifying you of changes beyond this page itself — material changes will be described here directly.
